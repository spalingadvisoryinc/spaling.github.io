Investigation & evidenceExample post

What does “accessed” actually tell us?

An audit event can look conclusive at first glance. The useful question is what the system recorded—and what that record actually allows us to conclude.

Read note

This is sample editorial content to demonstrate the Field Notes format, rather than a report of a client engagement.

The observation

A label such as “accessed” can invite a much broader interpretation than the available evidence supports. Before relying on it, we need to understand the event’s documented meaning, the application involved, and the surrounding activity.

Why it matters

Technical findings often become decisions about people, disclosure, and organizational risk. Confidence should follow the evidence, including its limits.

The takeaway: Separate what the record shows, what you infer, and what remains unknown.

GovernanceExample post

A control needs someone who owns it.

A policy can describe what should happen. The next question is who makes sure it actually does.

Read note

This is sample content to demonstrate a shorter Field Note.

A useful review goes beyond asking whether a control exists. It asks who operates it, who checks it, and what happens when it fails.

The takeaway: Make accountability as visible as the control itself.

AdvisoryExample post

The question behind the question.

“Is this secure?” is often the beginning of a conversation about what someone is trying to accomplish.

Read note

This is sample content to demonstrate a shorter Field Note.

Before assessing a technology, clarify the decision it needs to support. What information is involved? Who depends on it? What would a failure mean for them?

The takeaway: Start with the people and the decision, then work through the technology.
